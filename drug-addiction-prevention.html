Drug Addiction Prevention in Telangana

Substance use can affect physical health, mental well-being, education, employment and family relationships. Prevention begins with accurate information, supportive relationships, early assistance and access to appropriate healthcare.

Ashraya Welfare and Educational Society aims to promote awareness, encourage healthy choices and support compassionate community responses to substance-related problems.

Understanding the Risks of Substance Use

Alcohol, tobacco and other psychoactive substances can affect people differently. Some substances may cause dependence, impair judgement, increase accident risks and contribute to physical or mental health problems.

  • Problems with concentration, memory or decision-making.
  • Changes in school, college or work performance.
  • Financial difficulties and family conflict.
  • Increased risk of accidents and other health complications.
  • Difficulty reducing or stopping use despite harmful consequences.

A person experiencing substance-related problems deserves respectful support and professional care, not shame or discrimination.

Preventing Substance Use Among Young People

  • Provide age-appropriate, evidence-based awareness sessions.
  • Encourage positive friendships, sports, arts and other activities.
  • Help young people manage stress and peer pressure.
  • Encourage open communication between parents and children.
  • Connect people who need help with qualified healthcare providers.

Support for Families and Singareni Worker Communities

Families and working communities can play an important role in prevention by promoting awareness, encouraging healthy coping skills and helping people seek timely support.

Awareness activities may be organised in schools, colleges, workplaces and community settings, subject to the organisation's plans and available resources.

Families should avoid threats, humiliation and blame. Calm conversation and guidance from trained professionals can help people access care.

Treatment and Recovery

Substance use disorders are treatable health conditions. Depending on individual needs, care may include medical assessment, counselling, psychological treatment, supervised withdrawal management and rehabilitation.

Recovery can take time. Ongoing support from healthcare professionals, family members and community services may help a person maintain progress and rebuild relationships.

Do not stop or change prescribed medication without consulting a qualified healthcare professional.

Where to Get Help

If you or someone you know is experiencing problems related to substance use, contact a qualified doctor, mental health professional or recognised de-addiction treatment service.

In India, the government de-addiction helpline is 14446. Please verify current availability and services when contacting the helpline.

If someone is unconscious, having seizures, experiencing severe breathing difficulties or showing signs of an overdose, seek emergency medical assistance immediately.
